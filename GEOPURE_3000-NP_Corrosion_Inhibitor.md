# Geopure®3000-NP: Non-Phosphorus Corrosion Inhibitor (RO Permeate/DM Makeup)

> **Naming note:** this formulation is issued under a placeholder "3000-NP" designation in this draft because the code "Geopure 3000" is already assigned to your zinc/phosphonate, acidic-concentrate corrosion inhibitor for general hard-water cooling towers. That is a materially different chemistry (contains zinc, HEDP/phosphoric acid, operates at pH 2.5–3.5) from this product (zero zinc, zero phosphorus, operates at pH 7.5–8.0). Recommend a distinct final product code before release to avoid mis-shipment, mis-dosing, or SDS mix-ups in the field.

## Product Overview

Zero-zinc, zero-phosphorus, near-neutral pH corrosion inhibitor for cooling systems on **RO permeate or DM makeup**, where hardness/alkalinity are already stripped and the treatment burden is corrosion control rather than scale prevention. This program is positioned as the **environmentally-preferred alternative** to a phosphonate/zinc blend (e.g., Geopure 3005) for sites with tight phosphorus discharge limits, or — often the more compelling reason for a ZLD/MEE-adjacent site — because **phosphate carried in blowdown scales downstream RO reject and MEE condensers**, a problem this non-P chemistry avoids entirely regardless of what the local discharge limit actually requires.

Corrosion protection here is carried jointly by sodium molybdate (anodic passivation), sodium benzoate (ferrous-metal film formation), imidazoline (filming amine, general corrosion protection), and BTA (copper). PASP and the acrylic/AMPS polymer package provide dispersancy and residual threshold action for any incidental hardness ingress. Because there is no zinc, there is no zinc-solubility ceiling to manage at this pH — manufacturing is materially simpler than a zinc-containing neutral-pH product (contrast with Geopure 3005's staged pH-raise procedure).

Target performance: corrosion rate <3 mpy (mild steel), <0.1 mpy (copper). **Read the Dosage Guidelines note below before assuming this achieves equivalent protection to a zinc/phosphate program at the same product dose** — molybdate-only anodic protection typically needs a materially higher residual than a zinc-assisted system, and this composition's molybdate loading has real economic implications at high CoC (see below).

## Inhibition Mechanism

- **Anodic**: Molybdate (MoO₄²⁻) adsorbs onto anodic sites and reinforces the natural passive oxide layer on mild steel; sodium benzoate contributes a complementary organic film at ferrous-metal anodic sites
- **General/filming**: Imidazoline forms a hydrophobic, adsorbed film across the metal surface, providing broad-spectrum protection that partially substitutes for the cathodic role zinc would otherwise play
- **Copper protection**: BTA forms a chemisorbed Cu(I)-BTA film, preventing oxidative (blue-water) attack
- **Threshold/dispersion**: PASP acts as a biodegradable threshold inhibitor and dispersant; polyacrylic acid and AMPS/acrylate copolymer keep iron oxide and any incidental particulate matter suspended for blowdown removal

**Note on mechanism completeness:** without zinc or an orthophosphate/phosphonate component, this program has no dedicated cathodic precipitation inhibitor. Cathodic protection here relies on the combined film-forming action of imidazoline and the general passivation support molybdate provides — a real mechanism, but generally regarded in the field as offering a narrower margin than a zinc-assisted cathodic inhibitor, particularly under upset conditions (low residual, oxygen ingress, stagnant zones). Corrosion coupon data should be used to confirm this program meets target performance for your specific system before scaling to multiple sites.

## Manufacturing Procedure

### Batch Size: 100 kg

| Step | Action | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Charge 50 kg RO/DM water | – | Ambient |
| 2 | Start agitator at 100 RPM | – | – |
| 3 | Add 4.0 kg PASP | 10 min | Dissolve completely |
| 4 | Add 3.0 kg sodium gluconate | 10 min | Dissolve completely |
| 5 | Add 4.0 kg sodium benzoate | 10 min | Dissolve completely |
| 6 | Add 2.0 kg sodium molybdate | 10 min | Dissolve completely |
| 7 | Add 1.5 kg Imidazoline | 15 min | **Confirm supplier grade before charging.** Free-base imidazoline has poor water solubility, especially as the batch approaches the target pH 7.5–8.0. If using free-base material, partially neutralize with a small measured acid charge to form a water-soluble salt before blending in; if using a pre-neutralized/water-soluble salt grade or an aqueous emulsion, charge directly. If haze or a separate oily layer appears at this step, do not proceed to Step 11 until resolved |
| 8 | Add 0.5 kg BTA | 10 min | – |
| 9 | Add 1.75 kg polyacrylic acid (40–45% solution) | 10 min | – |
| 10 | Add 1.75 kg AMPS/acrylate copolymer (~40% solution) | 10 min | – |
| 11 | Slowly add sodium hydroxide (48% solution) to pH 7.5–8.0 | 15–20 min | Add in increments, check clarity after each. Estimated ~1.5 kg — confirm and record actual quantity on first production run |
| 12 | Makeup to 100 kg with RO/DM water | 5 min | Adds ~30 kg based on estimated NaOH charge |
| 13 | Mix 15 min | 15 min | Clear, light amber to straw-colored liquid *(color estimate — imidazoline-containing formulations often carry more color than a purely inorganic-salt blend; confirm on first batch)* |
| 14 | QC sampling | – | – |

**Mass balance check:** Steps 1–11 total ≈70.0 kg raw charge (based on the estimated NaOH quantity); Step 12 makeup adds the balance to close at 100 kg. Confirm the exact NaOH charge by titration on the first batch and update this SOP.

**Manufacturing advantage over a zinc-containing neutral-pH product:** because there is no zinc in this formulation, there is no solubility ceiling to manage during the pH-raise step — NaOH can be added without the incremental turbidity-checking discipline required for a zinc-based product like Geopure 3005. The one genuine risk in this process is Step 7 (imidazoline dispersion), not the pH adjustment.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Calculation Basis

Maintain in recirculating water:
- Molybdate: 40–75 mg/L as MoO₄ *(engineering assumption — see cost/CoC note below; verify against corrosion coupon performance)*
- Polymer: 2–4 mg/L actives

```
Product dose in makeup (mg/L) = Target recirculating concentration / (CoC × product active fraction)

Example:
Target MoO4 = 50 mg/L in recirc, CoC = 10, product MoO4 fraction = 0.0132
Makeup dose = 50 / (10 × 0.0132) = 379 mg/L product in makeup
```

**Important cost/design note — read before setting a program CoC:** at this formulation's molybdate loading (2.0% sodium molybdate, ≈1.32% as MoO₄), reaching a genuinely protective molybdate residual at CoC 10 requires **~380 mg/L product in makeup** — 2.5–3x higher than the typical 100–150 mg/L dose range seen on the phosphonate/zinc products in this line. Molybdenum is a volatile-priced commodity, so this has real program-economics consequences at scale, not just a chemistry footnote. Three ways to handle this, all legitimate — pick based on your commercial priorities, not mine:

1. **Increase the molybdate loading** in the composition (e.g., to 5–8% sodium molybdate) if this product is meant to run as a standalone anodic inhibitor at high CoC — this brings product dose back toward the 100–150 mg/L range
2. **Cap the operating CoC** at a more moderate level (5–8) to keep product dose and cost in a normal range, accepting a lower CoC than an RO/DM makeup could technically support
3. **Position this product for moderate corrosion-risk applications** and lean more on imidazoline/benzoate/PASP co-inhibition rather than treating molybdate as the primary residual to chase

The table below shows the actual dose/cost trade-off across CoC so you can make that call with real numbers rather than a rule of thumb.

### Dosing Point
- Makeup water line or cooling tower basin
- Continuous dosing via metering pump
- Use conductivity-based blowdown control to maintain CoC

## Application Methodology

### Startup (New System)
1. Fill system with RO permeate/DM water
2. Begin circulation
3. Slug dose at 3x normal rate for first 24 hours (passivation)
4. Reduce to maintenance dose per the molybdate-target calculation above
5. Begin biocide program after 24 hours

### Passivation Protocol
Initial film formation requires elevated molybdate and imidazoline film-former levels:
- Molybdate: 75–100 mg/L for 72 hours
- pH: 7.5–8.0
- After passivation, reduce to maintenance levels per the dosing calculation

## Safety Data Sheet (Summary)

### GHS Classification
- H315: Causes skin irritation *(near-neutral pH — not corrosive in the way an acidic concentrate is; confirm against actual finished-product testing)*
- H319: Causes serious eye irritation
- Molybdenum compounds: no standard aquatic-hazard pictogram equivalent to zinc's H410 at this concentration, but confirm against the actual sodium molybdate SDS and any site-specific discharge limits for molybdenum

### Environmental Note
No zinc, no phosphorus — this product avoids both the Zn discharge limit consideration (India CPCB Zn <5 mg/L) that applies to Geopure 2000/3000/3005, and any total-phosphorus discharge limit. Confirm local molybdenum discharge limits if applicable, as these vary by jurisdiction and are less universally standardized than zinc limits.

### PPE

## Storage and Handling

- HDPE containers preferred
- Temperature: 5–40°C
- Keep sealed to prevent evaporation and concentration increase
- **Microbial control note:** near-neutral pH plus an organic-rich formulation (PASP, gluconate, imidazoline) is more susceptible to microbial growth in storage than the acidic 2000/3000 concentrates. Recommend a low-dose preservative or a tightened shelf-life with routine microbial plate-count testing on stored drums/day tanks, same as noted for Geopure 3005.
- **Imidazoline phase stability:** monitor stored product periodically for any oily-layer separation or haze, particularly at the lower end of the storage temperature range, given the solubility caution raised in Manufacturing Procedure Step 7.
- Shelf life: 12 months (see notes above)

## Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Probable Cause | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| Oily film or haze on stored concentrate | Imidazoline not fully water-soluble/dispersed at this pH | Check supplier grade (free base vs. water-soluble salt vs. emulsion), review Step 7 |
| High corrosion rate on mild steel (>5 mpy) | Molybdate residual below effective threshold for a non-zinc/non-phosphate program | Check residual against the 40–75 mg/L MoO₄ target, increase dose — remember this product needs a materially higher residual than a zinc-based product to achieve equivalent protection |
| Localized/pitting attack despite adequate general corrosion coupon readings | Molybdate under-dosing risk is specifically a pitting-corrosion risk, not just general metal loss | Do not rely on general coupon weight-loss alone; inspect coupons for pitting, increase molybdate residual target |
| Copper corrosion (blue water) | BTA underdosed or depleted by oxidizing biocide | Verify BTA residual, increase product dose, moderate oxidizing biocide feed rate |
| Program cost higher than expected at target CoC | Molybdate loading in this composition requires a high product dose to reach protective residual at high CoC (see Dosage Guidelines cost note) | Reassess CoC target, or revisit molybdate loading in the composition per the three options given in Dosage Guidelines |
| Microbial growth / odor in stored concentrate | Near-neutral, organic-rich formulation lacks the self-preserving effect of acidic concentrates | Run plate count, consider preservative addition, reduce storage interval between batches |
