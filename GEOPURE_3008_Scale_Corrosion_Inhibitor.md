# Geopure® 3008: Scale & Corrosion Inhibitor for Open Circulation Systems on Softened Water Makeup

## 1. Product Overview

Seven-component scale and corrosion inhibitor combining four calcium-carbonate/calcium-phosphate threshold chemistries (PAPEMP, PBTC, HEDP, polymaleic acid) with three dispersant polymers (polyacrylic acid, terpolymer, AMPS copolymer), reinforced with zinc-phosphate cathodic protection and gluconate synergy — designed for open recirculating systems on makeup water carrying **pH 7.86, TDS 700–1000 ppm, Total Hardness up to 300 ppm as CaCO₃, Total Alkalinity 280+ ppm as CaCO₃, Chloride 140 ppm, LSI +0.75, RSI 6.36, PSI 6.09**.

**Naming note, resolved:** the 300 ppm hardness reflects softener slippage from improper regeneration, not a data inconsistency or a blended stream — with correct regeneration, hardness would run closer to 98 ppm. This is a genuine root-cause finding, not just a formulation-sizing detail: the softener's mechanical/operational performance, not the chemical program, is the primary lever available here. See Section 12 for the LSI impact of this gap and Section 26 for how to position it with the customer.

**Design basis:** raw-water LSI (+0.75) is only mildly scale-forming, but this water concentrates meaningfully under normal cooling tower CoC operation — see Dosing Based on Makeup Water Hardness for the projected at-CoC LSI trend, which is the actual justification for this formulation's heavier threshold-inhibitor loading relative to a fresh, low-hardness makeup product.

Target performance: corrosion rate <3 mpy (mild steel), calcium carbonate and calcium phosphate scale control through CoC 5–8 (pending confirmation of your actual target CoC against the projected LSI trend).

## 2. Product Features & Benefits

- Four-mechanism scale threshold protection (PAPEMP, PBTC, HEDP, polymaleic acid) sized for moderate-to-high CoC operation on hard, alkaline makeup
- PBTC provides chlorine/oxidizer stability where HEDP alone would degrade faster under an oxidizing biocide program
- Zinc-phosphate cathodic protection with gluconate-synergized molybdate-free* corrosion inhibition
- Broad dispersant coverage (PAA, terpolymer, AMPS copolymer) across calcium, iron, and general particulate fouling
- Single-product simplicity for a chemistry that would otherwise require blending multiple standalone treatments

*This formulation contains no molybdate — corrosion protection relies on zinc/phosphate/gluconate chemistry only. Confirm this is the intended design versus the molybdate-containing products elsewhere in this line (Geopure 2000/3000/3005).

## 5. Scientific Corrosion Inhibition Mechanism

- **PAPEMP**: broad-spectrum threshold inhibition, particular strength on calcium-phosphate scale
- **PBTC**: threshold inhibition with superior stability against chlorine/oxidizing biocides — the mechanistic reason to run PBTC alongside HEDP rather than HEDP alone in a system where oxidizing biocide is part of the program
- **HEDP**: classical CaCO₃/CaSO₄ threshold inhibition, good general-purpose performance where oxidizer exposure is lower
- **Polymaleic acid**: calcium carbonate threshold/dispersancy
- **Polyacrylic acid**: general particulate and metal-oxide dispersancy
- **Terpolymer (Maxinal 5400)**: carboxylate/sulfonate/nonionic multifunctional deposit control, zinc-compatible
- **AMPS copolymer (Maxinal 4200)**: supplementary dispersancy under high-TDS conditions — directly relevant given this makeup's 700–1000 ppm TDS
- **Zn²⁺**: cathodic inhibition at mild steel surfaces
- **Sodium gluconate**: metal-ion complexation and general corrosion synergy

**No molybdate in this formulation** — anodic protection relies on the zinc-phosphate film rather than molybdate passivation. If field coupon data shows inadequate anodic protection, molybdate addition is the most direct fix, consistent with the pattern used elsewhere in this product line.

## 10. Dosage Guidelines

### Calculation Basis
```
Product dose in makeup (mg/L) = Target recirculating concentration / (CoC × product active fraction)
```
Given the zinc fraction here is the same dilute figure as Geopure 2003 CH/3004/3007 (≈0.00064), zinc is very unlikely to be the dose-limiting parameter — **dose to phosphonate/threshold-inhibitor residual**, consistent with the pattern established for this makeup-water-driven product family.

## 11. Dosage Calculation Examples

**Example 1 — targeting total phosphonate (PBTC+HEDP as PO₄) residual:**
```
Target = 10 mg/L, CoC = 5, product phosphonate fraction ≈ 0.05
Makeup dose = 10 / (5 × 0.05) = 40 mg/L product in makeup
```

**Example 2 — same target at CoC 8 (higher concentration factor, less product needed per unit makeup):**
```
Makeup dose = 10 / (8 × 0.05) = 25 mg/L product in makeup
```

**Example 3 — checking resulting Zn residual at the Example 1 dose:**
```
Zn residual = 40 mg/L × 5 × 0.00064 = 0.13 mg/L
```
This confirms zinc residual at a phosphonate-driven dose is well below the 1–2 mg/L target — consistent with treating phosphonate, not zinc, as the controlling parameter. If a specific Zn residual is required for cathodic protection, that will need a materially higher product dose than the phosphonate target alone suggests — worth deciding which parameter actually governs before finalizing a standard dosing chart.

## 12. Dosing Based on Makeup Water Hardness

*Reinterpreted per the pattern established across this product line — dose is corrosion/scale-limited, not simply hardness-driven. Using your stated raw-water chemistry, the projected at-CoC LSI trend (method: holding pH constant as a conservative floor, recalculating pHs from concentrated hardness/alkalinity — see Product Overview for the derivation) is the real basis for setting a CoC ceiling:*

| CoC | Projected TH (ppm CaCO₃) | Projected Alkalinity (ppm CaCO₃) | Projected Chloride (ppm) | Estimated LSI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (raw) | 300 | 280 | 140 | +0.75 |
| 3 | 900 | 840 | 420 | ~+1.70 |
| 5 | 1,500 | 1,400 | 700 | ~+2.15 |
| 8 | 2,400 | 2,240 | 1,120 | ~+2.56 |

**This is an approximation, not a substitute for your own LSI/RSI/PSI calculator.** Real systems typically see pH climb further with CO₂ stripping at higher CoC, meaning actual LSI at a given CoC is likely somewhat higher than shown here. Chloride alone reaches 700 ppm at CoC 5 — a real constraint if any stainless steel is present in the system, independent of the scaling question.

**Recommended maximum CoC, pending your confirmation:** 4–5 under current (degraded) softener performance, balancing the LSI trend against the chloride trend — both become significant constraints in the same CoC range for this water.

**The bigger lever is the softener, not the chemical program.** Since the 300 ppm hardness reflects improper regeneration rather than the water's actual post-softening potential, the same projection run against the corrected 98 ppm baseline (alkalinity unaffected, since ion exchange doesn't touch it) looks meaningfully different:

| CoC | LSI — current (300 ppm, improper regeneration) | LSI — corrected (98 ppm, proper regeneration) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (raw) | +0.75 | ~+0.26 |
| 3 | ~+1.70 | ~+1.21 |
| 5 | ~+2.15 | ~+1.66 |
| 8 | ~+2.56 | ~+2.07 |

Roughly half an LSI unit of headroom is sitting in the softener fix at every CoC level. Practically, this means: **root-cause action on regeneration (salt dosing, brine concentration, regeneration frequency/timing, resin capacity check) is likely worth more than any adjustment to this formulation.** Two honest options once that's raised with the customer — fix the softener and potentially operate at a higher CoC for better water/cost economics with the same chemical program, or fix the softener and reassess whether this formulation's heavy threshold-inhibitor loading could be right-sized down at the lower scaling demand. Either way, this formulation as built is correctly sized for *today's* actual condition (300 ppm) — the recommendation is to fix the mechanical issue in parallel, not to treat the current formulation as the permanent answer to a softener performance problem.

### Dosing Point

- Conductivity-based blowdown control to maintain the confirmed CoC target

## 13. Startup Application Procedure

1. Fill system with available makeup water
2. Begin circulation
3. Slug dose at 3x normal rate for 24–48 hours (passivation)
4. Reduce to maintenance dose per Section 11
5. Confirm actual operating CoC against the Section 12 projection using a real conductivity/chloride reading, not just the design assumption

## 14. Passivation Protocol
- Zinc: elevated to 4–6 mg/L for 72 hours
- Total phosphonate: elevated to 20–30 mg/L for 72 hours
- pH: hold at the confirmed system operating pH target
- After passivation, reduce to maintenance levels per Section 11

## 25. Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I run this above CoC 5?** Only after confirming actual LSI/chloride at the higher CoC against real water data — the projections in Section 12 show both becoming significant constraints beyond CoC 5 for this specific makeup water.

**Why no molybdate?** This formulation relies on zinc-phosphate anodic protection instead. If coupon data shows inadequate anodic protection, molybdate supplementation is the direct fix.

**Is this compatible with my existing biocide program?** Yes for both oxidizing and non-oxidizing biocides — PBTC was specifically included for oxidizer stability.

**What if the makeup water isn't actually softened?** Use the stated chemistry numbers, not the "softened" label, for all dosing and CoC decisions — see the naming note in Product Overview.

Position this as the **CoC-optimized program for moderate-hardness, moderate-alkalinity makeup** — the differentiator versus Geopure 2000/3000 is the four-mechanism threshold package sized specifically for this water's projected at-CoC scaling behavior, not a generic higher-dose version of a standard product. Lead with the LSI-at-CoC analysis (Section 12) in customer conversations — it demonstrates root-cause sizing rather than product-pushing, consistent with a program-based rather than product-selling conversation. Be prepared to address the "why seven ingredients" question directly using the same analysis — it's a legitimate question and the CoC-trend data is the honest answer.

**Lead with the softener finding, not the chemical sale.** The current 300 ppm hardness slippage is a genuine root-cause issue independent of this product — raising it first (before pitching the formulation) is exactly the diagnostic, non-product-pushing positioning this company wants to be known for. The honest sequence is: (1) flag the regeneration issue and recommend it be corrected regardless of what chemical program is chosen, (2) size this formulation for the water as it actually is today, (3) note explicitly that once the softener is fixed, the program should be revisited — either for a higher operating CoC at the same chemical cost, or a lighter formulation at the same CoC. Raising point 3 unprompted is a credibility move, not a revenue risk — a customer who sees you flag a path to *less* chemical spend trusts the rest of the recommendation more.
