## 1. Purpose

This document provides general technical guidance for the selection, application, dosing, monitoring and control of GEOPURE® cooling water treatment chemicals.

The treatment program is designed to control:

- Scale formation
- Corrosion
- Microbiological growth
- Biofilm and slime
- Suspended solids and deposits
- High conductivity/TDS
- Under-deposit corrosion
- Heat-transfer deterioration
- Excessive water consumption

The treatment program should be selected based on **make-up water chemistry, metallurgy, operating temperature, cycles of concentration, system volume, heat load and microbiological conditions**.

---

# 2. Cooling Water Treatment Chemistry

A complete cooling-water program normally consists of four control areas:

| Treatment Area | Primary Objective |
|---|---|
| Scale control | Prevent calcium carbonate, calcium phosphate, sulfate and silica-related deposits |
| Corrosion control | Protect carbon steel, GI, copper and copper alloys |
| Microbiological control | Control bacteria, algae, fungi and biofilm |
| Water-quality control | Maintain conductivity/TDS and dissolved solids within operating limits |

No single chemical should be expected to provide optimum control under all operating conditions.

# 4. Microbiological Control

Cooling towers provide favorable conditions for microbial growth because of:

- Warm water
- Oxygen availability
- Nutrients
- Large surface area
- Continuous recirculation
- Atmospheric contamination

Typical microbiological treatment may include:

### Oxidizing biocides

- Sodium hypochlorite
- Calcium hypochlorite
- Chlorine dioxide
- Bromine-based oxidants

### Non-oxidizing biocides

- DBNPA
- Isothiazolinones
- Quaternary ammonium compounds
- Other application-specific microbiocides

Biocide selection must consider pH, temperature, organic loading, system metallurgy and compatibility with the corrosion/scale inhibitor program.

# 5. Conductivity and Blowdown Control

As water evaporates, dissolved salts remain in the cooling-water system.

Therefore:

**Cycles of Concentration (COC) = Cooling Water Concentration / Make-up Water Concentration**

For conductivity-based control:

**COC ≈ Cooling Water Conductivity / Make-up Water Conductivity**

The relationship is approximate because conductivity is affected by the ionic composition and treatment chemicals.

Automatic conductivity-controlled blowdown is preferred over continuous uncontrolled bleed-off where practical.

# 7. Cooling Tower Chemical Dosing

## 7.1 Initial System Treatment

For a new or heavily contaminated system:

1. Inspect cooling tower basin.
2. Clean basin and accessible surfaces.
3. Check fill condition.
4. Inspect nozzles and distribution system.
5. Flush dead legs.
6. Check heat exchanger/condenser condition.
7. Fill with make-up water.
8. Establish required water volume.
9. Add initial scale/corrosion inhibitor charge.
10. Establish microbiological control.
11. Start circulation.
12. Verify pH and conductivity.
13. Start controlled blowdown.
14. Establish routine dosing.

Initial dosing should be based on **system volume and product active concentration**, not simply a fixed kg/day value.

---

# 8. Continuous Dosing

A typical program consists of:

### Scale/Corrosion Inhibitor

Continuous proportional dosing based on:

- Make-up water flow
- Circulation rate
- COC
- System volume
- Product concentration
- Water chemistry

### Biocide

Applied either:

- Continuously at low level, or
- Intermittently as a slug dose

depending on the microbiological program.

### Blowdown

Controlled using:

- Conductivity
- TDS
- Manual testing
- Automatic conductivity controller

Automatic dosing and blowdown control provides substantially better process stability than manual chemical addition.

# 9. Basic Chemical Dosing Calculation

For a liquid product:

**Daily Product Requirement (kg/day) = Make-up Water (m³/day) × Dose (g/m³) ÷ 1000**

Example:

Make-up water = 50 m³/day  
Required product dose = 40 g/m³

Therefore:

**Product requirement = 50 × 40 ÷ 1000**

**= 2.0 kg/day**

For metering-pump calibration:

**Pump Output (L/h) = Required Daily Product Volume ÷ Operating Hours/day**

Product density must be considered when converting kg/day to L/day.

# 10. Chemical Feed Points

Recommended feed locations should provide adequate mixing before the treated water reaches the heat exchanger.

Typical locations include:

### Scale/Corrosion Inhibitor

Cooling tower basin or recirculation line, preferably at a location providing rapid dispersion.

### Biocide

Feed point should provide adequate distribution throughout the cooling system.

### Acid

Acid dosing should be independently controlled with appropriate pH/conductivity instrumentation and interlocks.

Avoid uncontrolled mixing of concentrated treatment chemicals.

- Corrosion coupons

- Iron monitoring
- Copper monitoring
- Visual inspection
- Deposit analysis

Typical corrosion monitoring targets must be established according to metallurgy and system design.

# 13. Microbiological Monitoring

Monitor:

- Total bacterial count
- Algae
- Fungal growth
- Slime
- Biofilm
- Legionella risk where applicable

Chemical treatment does not eliminate the need for mechanical cleaning and good tower hygiene.

Cooling towers should be maintained to minimize stagnant zones and organic accumulation.

---

# 14. Blowdown Control

The purpose of blowdown is to remove concentrated dissolved and suspended contaminants.

Excessive blowdown causes:

- Increased water consumption
- Increased chemical consumption
- Higher wastewater generation
- Increased operating cost

Insufficient blowdown can result in:

- High conductivity
- Scale formation
- Corrosion
- Increased chloride concentration
- Silica accumulation
- Poor heat transfer

The optimum blowdown rate should be established from water chemistry and COC requirements.

# 17. Troubleshooting Matrix

| Problem | Possible Cause | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| High conductivity | Insufficient blowdown | Check conductivity controller and blowdown valve |
| Rapid scale formation | High COC, hardness or alkalinity | Optimize COC and scale inhibitor |
| Corrosion | Inadequate inhibitor, low pH, high chloride or oxygen | Review chemistry and corrosion monitoring |
| Algae | Insufficient biocide/light exposure | Optimize biocide program and tower hygiene |
| Slime | Poor microbiological control | Shock treatment and review biocide program |
| High iron | Corrosion or external contamination | Investigate corrosion source |
| Copper corrosion | Poor azole control or aggressive water | Review copper-inhibitor program |
| High chemical consumption | Overdosing or excessive blowdown | Calibrate dosing and optimize COC |
| Poor heat transfer | Scale, biofilm or suspended solids | Inspect exchanger and cooling tower |
| High TDS | Excessive concentration | Increase controlled blowdown |
| Low Delta-T | Poor heat transfer or excessive flow | Inspect exchanger, tower and process conditions |

# 18. Chemical Compatibility

Before combining chemicals, evaluate:

- pH
- Active concentration
- Ionic compatibility
- Oxidant compatibility
- Metal compatibility
- Storage stability
- Temperature stability
- Biocide compatibility
- Polymer compatibility

Do not mix concentrated oxidizing biocides directly with concentrated reducing agents, acids, alkalis or other incompatible chemicals.

Chemical compatibility must be verified through laboratory and field testing.

# 19. Storage

General storage requirements:

- Store in compatible HDPE containers.
- Keep containers tightly closed.
- Protect from direct sunlight.
- Store in a cool, ventilated chemical area.
- Prevent freezing where applicable.
- Keep incompatible chemicals segregated.
- Maintain secondary containment.
- Follow the product-specific SDS.

Always use the current product SDS for storage, PPE and emergency requirements.

# 21. Important Safety Rule

Never assume two cooling-water treatment products are compatible merely because both are used in the same system.

In particular, evaluate compatibility between:

- Oxidizing biocides
- Non-oxidizing biocides
- Phosphonates
- Polymers
- Azoles
- Zinc compounds
- Molybdates
- Acids
- Alkalis
- Reducing agents

Follow the individual SDS and technical data sheet for every product.

# 22. Recommended Treatment Philosophy

An effective cooling-water program should be based on:

**Water Analysis → System Survey → Treatment Selection → Controlled Dosing → Blowdown Control → Online Monitoring → Performance Verification**

The objective is not maximum chemical dosing.

The objective is **minimum total operating cost while maintaining acceptable scale, corrosion and microbiological control**.

Optimization should consider:

- Water cost
- Chemical cost
- Wastewater cost
- Energy consumption
- Heat-transfer efficiency
- Equipment life
- Maintenance frequency
- Downtime risk
