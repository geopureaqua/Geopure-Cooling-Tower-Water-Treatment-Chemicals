# Geopure® 3004: Green-Oriented Multi-Metal Corrosion Inhibitor for Open Recirculating and Chilled-Water Systems

## Product Overview

Organic-rich corrosion inhibitor incorporating imidazole, sodium benzoate, gluconate, and biodegradable polymeric dispersants (PASP + terpolymer), synergistically reinforced with molybdate and controlled zinc-phosphate chemistry, for protection of carbon steel, galvanized iron (GI), copper, and brass across two chemically distinct applications: **open recirculating cooling towers** (O₂ ingress + evaporative concentration + iron corrosion products + suspended solids + biological/deposit environment) and **open chilled-water systems** (O₂ ingress + mixed metallurgy + low-temperature corrosion + stagnant sections + makeup-water contamination). The multi-mechanistic package is deliberately sized to serve both duty profiles rather than being optimized narrowly for one.

**Marketing/labeling constraint — carried through this entire document:** this product **contains zinc and phosphate**. Do not claim non-phosphorus, phosphate-free, zinc-free, 100% biodegradable, or 100% green anywhere this formulation is referenced. The defensible positioning is: *organic-rich, multi-metal corrosion inhibitor with reduced zinc/phosphate reliance relative to a conventional zinc-phosphonate program* — not a fully green claim.

**pH discipline for GI protection:** target pH is held at **8.0–8.5, deliberately not pushed to 9+**. Zinc surfaces behave differently from carbon steel — a high-pH environment combined with excessive alkalinity, zinc, phosphate, and certain polymer chemistries can contribute to zinc-surface attack (white rust) rather than protecting it. This constraint is especially important wherever the system contains significant GI, and is treated as a hard ceiling throughout this document, not a target to optimize past.

Target corrosion rates:

| Metallurgy | Target |
|---|---|
| Carbon steel | <2 mpy |
| GI | Minimal measurable attack / no white rust |
| Copper | <0.2–0.5 mpy |
| Brass | <0.2–0.5 mpy |

## Inhibition Mechanism

- **Imidazole** → Cu/brass surface adsorption *(mechanism claim pending raw-material identity confirmation — see note above; if the actual material is an imidazoline or benzimidazole/MBI derivative, this description is broadly correct; if it is literally unsubstituted imidazole, this claim should be reassessed against actual coupon performance data before being used in customer-facing material)*
- **Sodium molybdate** → anodic steel passivation, with gluconate acting as a documented synergist that improves molybdate's passivation efficiency at lower total dose
- **Sodium benzoate** → complementary organic inhibitor for carbon steel; its direct contribution to GI (zinc surface) protection specifically is less established in the literature than its steel role — GI protection in this formulation should be attributed primarily to the pH-window discipline (8.0–8.5) and the zinc/phosphate balance, not to benzoate's action on zinc directly
- **Zn²⁺** (from the zinc-phosphate solution) → cathodic inhibition, precipitating as Zn(OH)₂/zinc phosphate film at cathodic sites
- **Sodium gluconate** → metal-ion complexation and molybdate synergy (see above)
- **PASP + terpolymer** → dispersion of iron corrosion products and deposits — sized deliberately high given both target applications generate meaningful particulate load (evaporative concentration in the cooling-tower case, sustained O₂ ingress in the chilled-water case)
- **Sodium bisulfite** → supplemental reduction of dissolved oxygen demand; explicitly supplemental rather than the primary O₂ control mechanism, which is the correct framing — full reliance on an uncatalyzed bisulfite reaction against continuous, high-rate O₂ ingress would face the same kinetic limitations noted for Geopure 2003 CH

## Dosage Guidelines

### Calculation Basis

**Per your own framing — do not control this product by ppm-of-product alone. Use the control matrix below, coupon/probe-verified, not a single-parameter dose calculation.**

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| pH | 8.0–8.5 |
| Imidazole | 5–15 mg/L |
| Zn | 1–2 mg/L as Zn (maintenance); consider 3–4 mg/L during passivation — see Passivation Protocol |
| Mo | 5–10 mg/L as Mo initially (passivation); 2–4 mg/L as Mo maintenance *(maintenance figure is a flagged placeholder — optimize against carbon-steel and copper coupon data as you intended, not by further increasing concentration)* |
| Dissolved oxygen | Trend — no fixed target, track direction |
| Chloride | Preferably <100–150 mg/L |
| Total iron | Trend |
| Dissolved copper | Trend |
| Conductivity / CoC | Site-specific — derived from makeup-water chemistry (Ca hardness, alkalinity, silica, chloride, sulfate, temperature, pH), not from this formulation. This is deliberately not a formulation-level parameter. |
| Corrosion rate | Coupon/probe controlled |

**Example dose calculation (Mo, as Mo — illustrative only):**
```
Product dose in makeup (mg/L) = Target recirculating concentration / (CoC × product active fraction)

Target Mo = 7 mg/L (mid-passivation range), CoC = 5, product Mo fraction = 0.0159
Makeup dose = 7 / (5 × 0.0159) = 88 mg/L product in makeup
```
Note this is one input into the control matrix, not the sole control variable — confirm actual Zn, imidazole, and corrosion-rate readings track acceptably at whatever dose the Mo calculation suggests.

### Dosing by Application (Maximum Level — corrosion-limited, not scale-limited)

*This product serves two distinct applications with different dosing logic — presented separately rather than forcing one framework onto both, consistent with your own point that CoC/hardness limits are site-specific and separate from the formulation itself.*

**Open recirculating cooling tower:** CoC ceiling is derived from the site's actual makeup-water analysis (hardness, alkalinity, silica, chloride, sulfate, temperature, pH) — not a generic table built into this document. Once CoC is established from that analysis, dose per the calculation basis above against the chloride ceiling (<100–150 mg/L in recirculating water) as the practical corrosion-driven limit, alongside the Mo/Zn/imidazole targets.

**Open chilled-water system:** treat as a fixed-volume system with periodic top-up (no continuous blowdown/CoC mechanism in most such systems) — initial charge based on system volume to hit the control-matrix targets, top-up tied to measured water loss and residual decline, consistent with the Geopure 2003 CH dosing approach.

### Dosing Point
- **Cooling tower:** makeup water line or cooling tower basin, continuous dosing via metering pump, conductivity-based blowdown control to maintain CoC
- **Chilled water:** shot-feed via pot feeder/chemical feed tank into the return line or at the expansion/surge tank, periodic rather than continuous

## Application Methodology

### Startup (New System)
1. Fill system with fresh water (or DM/RO for chilled-water applications)
2. Begin circulation
3. Slug dose to passivation-level targets (Mo 5–10 mg/L as Mo, Zn 3–4 mg/L) for the first 48–72 hours
4. Reduce to maintenance dosing per the control matrix
5. Begin biocide program after 24 hours (cooling tower) or establish DO monitoring baseline (chilled water)

### Passivation Protocol
- Molybdenum: 5–10 mg/L as Mo for 72 hours (per your stated startup target)
- Zinc: elevated to 3–4 mg/L for 72 hours *(flagged assumption — confirm against coupon data)*
- pH: hold at 8.0–8.5 throughout — do not exceed 8.5 even transiently during passivation
- After passivation, reduce to maintenance levels per the control matrix

## Storage and Handling

- HDPE containers only
- Temperature: 5–35°C
- Keep sealed, minimize headspace air — bisulfite content makes this air-sensitive in storage
- **Critical incompatibility warning:** never store or mix this product near acid-based descaling products (Geoclean 1050, etc.) — bisulfite reacts with acid to release SO₂ gas
- Shelf life: 9 months — re-assay bisulfite residual before use on stock nearing this age

## Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Probable Cause | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| White rust / GI attack | pH drifted above 8.5, or zinc/phosphate/polymer balance has shifted | Check pH control point immediately — this is the primary safeguard for GI in this formulation; do not attempt to fix by adding more product |
| Copper corrosion (blue water) | Imidazole underdosed, or (pending identity confirmation) the raw material is not providing the expected Cu-film mechanism | Verify imidazole residual against the 5–15 mg/L target; if corrosion persists despite adequate residual, revisit raw-material identity per the open confirmation item |
| High corrosion rate on carbon steel | Low Mo/Zn residual, or pH excursion outside 8.0–8.5 | Check residuals against control matrix, verify pH |
| High iron (dissolved or particulate) | Elevated O₂ ingress outpacing dispersant capacity, or bisulfite exhausted | Confirm dispersant residual adequate; check DO trend; remember bisulfite here is supplemental, not primary O₂ control |
| Program cost or performance not matching expectations at high CoC (cooling tower use) | CoC set without reference to actual makeup-water chemistry | Re-derive CoC ceiling from a full makeup-water analysis (hardness, alkalinity, silica, chloride, sulfate, temperature, pH) — this is a site-specific input, not something this formulation document can supply generically |
| SO₂ odor / gas evolution during storage or handling | Product contaminated with or stored near an acidic product | Stop use immediately, ventilate — do not continue dosing from a compromised drum |
